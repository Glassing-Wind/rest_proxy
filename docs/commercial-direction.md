# Commercial direction and next steps — October 6, 2026

The user's need for income is a product constraint. The recommended next direction
is a small paid repository-investigation service built on the existing FIRE work,
with a source-graded demonstration before offering it. Preserve CLARITY as the
longer-term public-interest/personal-assistant direction. This is a recommendation
and testable offer, not evidence of paying customers, product-market fit or income.

Assumptions pending the user's preferences: near-term income matters more than a
long speculative platform build; start with one client and a bounded service; no
hardware purchase, public network launch or hosted SaaS deployment is required.
The stated target is $3,000/month. Whether that means gross revenue or take-home,
the deadline and available weekly delivery time remain unknown. The user reports
10,000+ contacts, but buyer needs are unqualified, and owns rental-platform and
rental-law repositories. Local paths have been identified; the rental applicant
UI now has a bounded demonstration. Broader implementation and redistribution
rights remain unassessed. Actual outreach, prices, contracts and invoicing are user decisions.

This ranking is an inference from our implementation evidence and execution burden,
not a measured market ranking. Sourcegraph publicly sells code understanding,
MCP/API access and related enterprise tools; its pricing page lists a $16,000
minimum annual enterprise contract as checked October 6. That establishes an
existing commercial category and strong competition, not demand for our offer or
permission to borrow enterprise claims. [Primary pricing source](https://sourcegraph.com/pricing).

Meta's current developer page offers mobile toolkit, web-app and AI-connector
paths for glasses. Access still needs device/version/terms acceptance; the landing
page does not prove our chosen capture or audio flow works. Our glasses application
has no implemented capture acceptance. [Meta developer paths](https://developers.meta.com/wearables/).

The economic distinction is practical: a human-reviewed investigation report can
be scoped and sold before a broadly supported installed platform is ready. Keep
software redistribution, managed installation and persistent hosting out of the
first offer. Customer code still requires explicit access and processing agreement;
using software internally does not eliminate its license obligations.

## First buyer, problem and offer

Target hypothesis: a small software agency or engineering team maintaining a Python
application whose original author is unavailable or whose next change has unclear
impact. The buyer is the owner/engineering lead responsible for that change.

Offer a fixed-scope investigation of one repository and one concrete issue or
change, answering up to three agreed questions. Deliver a source-cited explanation,
relationship/change-impact map, a bounded plan and a live walkthrough with unresolved
questions clearly marked. The [reviewable pilot offer](paid-investigation-pilot.md)
is a draft the user can adapt before sharing. No messages have been sent.

A $750 fixed-price pilot is a **pricing hypothesis**, not a market average or an
agreed quote. Cap investigation/report effort at eight working hours, subject to a
pre-sale fit check. If the work cannot fit, reduce scope or decline before accepting
payment. Suggested 50% start payment is also a draft commercial term for the user
to approve. Do not promise a patch, security certification or complete graph coverage.
A separately scoped supervised patch/setup service may follow when requested.

Two $750 pilots produce $1,500 gross receipts, not take-home income. Four produce
$3,000 gross, with up to 32 investigation hours plus sales, administration and
rework. Track direct compute costs and all labor. Effective contribution per hour
is `(collected revenue - direct delivery costs) / all hours`, not headline price
or theoretical token savings. No guarantee of bookings or collection is implied.

Prefer the user's rental-platform repository for the first demonstration once its
path and access are confirmed. Trace one actual workflow, identify change impact
and document a reproducible defect or maintenance question with source citations.
The rental-law repository can demonstrate evidence provenance: jurisdiction,
effective date, primary authority, conflicting sources and explicit unknowns.
It must not turn retrieved text into an unreviewed promise of legal compliance.
The rental platform now has an initial local UI assessment and fix; see
[the user-testing milestone](agent-team-user-testing.md). Rental-law source and
full backend user journeys remain unassessed.

TurboTenant is a possible prospect, not an established buyer or partner. Its
published product includes applications, rent collection, maintenance and
state-specific leases, so a rental-domain demonstration could be relevant. That
is an inference, not evidence it needs this service or wants either repository.
[TurboTenant product scope](https://www.turbotenant.com/) (checked October 6, 2026).
Prepare a specific useful finding before proposing a conversation; keep other
agencies and rental-software teams in the candidate pool.

Treat the 10,000+ contacts as relationships, not a qualified sales pipeline. Manually
shortlist 15–20 people by known role, relationship and plausible problem. Record
what is actually known, who can introduce a decision-maker, and whether further
contact is welcome. Start with three problem conversations. Do not bulk import,
scrape or message the network; no outreach is authorized or performed here.

Keep the Meta developer path as a separate bounded experiment. The user reports

Protect capacity for outside-life responsibilities. Plan one small revenue sprint

The existing hourly automation may prepare local evidence and drafts, but cannot
supply customer conversations or make commercial commitments for the user.

## Thirty-day validation sequence

| Stage | Work | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–3 | Create one source-graded demonstration on our own or an expressly permitted repository. Answer three representative questions, include a change plan and record effort. | A reviewable report with independently checked citations; correct findings and explicit unknowns. |
| Days 4–7 | User selects a short list of known potential buyers and holds problem interviews. Draft messages may be prepared, but sending requires explicit user instruction. | Three conversations documenting the last real investigation problem, cost, current workflow and decision-maker. |
| Days 8–14 | Present the fixed-scope offer to qualified buyers and seek one paid pilot. Confirm questions, access, processing location and acceptance before work. | One agreed scope and collected start payment, or a documented refusal/reason. |
| Days 15–21 | Deliver the supervised pilot, have the buyer check findings and conduct a walkthrough. | Accepted deliverables, measured total effort, remaining questions and customer feedback. |
| Days 22–30 | Offer another pilot or a recurring investigation/support engagement only if the first was useful. | Repeat paid demand and known delivery cost; otherwise revise buyer/problem/offer. |

These are relative planning windows, not scheduled promises. A sale is not an
engineering acceptance gate: don't label an interested conversation a customer
or an unpaid demo a paid pilot. If ten qualified conversations produce no paid
interest, change the offer/problem rather than adding more graph features. If
scope or rework consumes the delivery budget, reduce scope before repeating it.

## Engineering work that supports revenue

The next concrete milestone is a **source-graded pilot demonstration and reusable
report template**, not another broad capability expansion. Start from the
[reviewable investigation template](templates/investigation-report.md). Use native source reads
for known files; use relationships/semantic discovery only when useful. Count
fallbacks, omissions, actual usage and total investigation/review effort. Include
indexing/setup cost separately so repeated-query speed does not hide onboarding cost.

Controlled paired coding-task evaluation remains required for claims of better
quality, fewer tokens or lower end-to-end latency. Prior pilot evidence establishes
no MCP superiority. A truthful service offer sells the agreed investigation outcome;
it does not advertise unmeasured savings or automated correctness.

Resolve release gates when the intended delivery requires them. A customer-installed
product still needs exact native/grammar/model notice closure, platform acceptance,
operational backup/deletion policy and support boundaries. Those gates remain in
[the five-priority status](five-priority-status.md); the service track does not
waive them. Don't exhaust the near-term effort auditing every unused grammar before
checking whether anyone wants the bounded investigation service.

## The larger mission

Keep [CLARITY's relationship/team/contact/access concepts](clarity-cockpit-direction.md)
and [the separate glasses direction](smart-glasses-project-direction.md) intact.
The eventual cockpit could help people understand and act on consented evidence.
Proposed revenue is from useful delivery, setup, support and team workflows; it is
not from selling private relationship graphs or making civic evidence accessible
only to paying institutions. That principle still needs a future operating model.
